# Impact pathway and proposed field pilot

This is a proposal, not a completed field study or a claim of ecological or health benefit.

## The intended contribution

StreamSignal supports urban freshwater monitoring by making the chain from environmental trigger to investigation explicit and auditable. Weather prediction is one input. The product's intended contribution is to help a monitoring team identify a testable water-ecosystem question, choose candidate visits under a stated budget, and preserve the measurements needed to evaluate that question.

| Stage | Product output | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Anticipate a condition | Dated 3- or 7-day weather-event probability | Evaluated retrospectively against recorded weather |
| Define an investigation | Ecological question, mechanism reference and proposed checklist | Analyst-authored; not a validated site-specific protocol |
| Allocate attention | Sites, coordinates, budget and transparent priority rule | Implemented; ecological effectiveness not evaluated |
| Collect water evidence | Unit-labelled field measurements, methods and contextual notes | Recording workflow implemented; no prospective study data collected |
| Interpret and act | Qualified review of water measurements and locally appropriate action | Outside the automated model; no safety declaration |
| Improve ecosystem / human-health decisions | Potentially better-informed monitoring and follow-up | Intended downstream benefit; not demonstrated |

General rationale references: [EPA stormwater](https://www.epa.gov/nutrientpollution/sources-and-solutions-stormwater), [USGS dissolved oxygen](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/dissolved-oxygen-and-water), and [EPA dissolved oxygen mechanisms](https://www.epa.gov/caddis/dissolved-oxygen). These references support mechanisms, not the tool's effectiveness or numerical planning rules.

## Proposed pilot question

Under the same completed-visit budget, does forecast-guided scheduling identify more pre-specified, verified water-quality changes than a routine monitoring schedule, without an unacceptable increase in missed changes or team workload?

## Design decisions to fix before collecting outcomes

1. Select a small set of study streams with the responsible monitoring organization. Verify weather provenance, actual availability time and whether the required observations exist before the intended forecast issue time.
2. Choose one investigation type and define its measurable water endpoint before the pilot. For a rainfall study, a partner might select a turbidity or nutrient-change endpoint with a documented reference/baseline and assay method. The partner must set the actual numerical criterion; this document invents no ecological or health cutoff.
3. Specify forecast thresholds, visit budgets, comparison schedule, sampling timing, reference locations, measurement methods, instrument checks and handling of missing visits before reviewing outcomes.
4. Use an allocation scheme agreed with a statistician and field partner, such as randomized monitoring blocks or matched stream-period comparisons. Treat shared-weather groups and overlapping weather episodes as clusters; do not count duplicated site forecasts or overlapping windows as independent evidence.
5. Include a scheduled reference-monitoring component that does not depend on model flags. Sampling only alerted sites cannot reveal the changes the model missed. Keep comparison effort explicit in the budget accounting.
6. Apply the same measurement procedures and quality-control rules to both schedules. Blind laboratory processing or outcome review to scheduling strategy where feasible. Distinguish true zero readings from unmeasured values.
7. Freeze model updates during the evaluation. Choose sample size and uncertainty analysis using plausible event frequency, clustering and the partner's minimum useful effect, rather than claiming a convenient number of visits is sufficient.

## Outcomes to report

**Primary candidate:** verified, pre-specified water-quality changes identified per ten completed visits, with uncertainty and the underlying numerator/denominator for each strategy. Endpoint definition and analysis must be finalized with the field partner before collection.

**Secondary outcomes:** time from qualifying change to documented review where independently observable; missed qualifying changes in reference monitoring; completed versus planned visits; staff time and travel burden; usable measurement-record proportion; and reasons for missing or unusable records.

A statistically or practically weak result should be reported. More alerts, higher weather AUC, or more notebook entries alone do not establish environmental impact. Do not infer reduced illness from water-monitoring improvements without an appropriate separate study.

## Current implementation gap

The notebook stores basic water measurements and free-text method/notes. A real study may also require laboratory results, sample identifiers, instrument/calibration records, reviewer sign-off, access controls and a shared research database. These are pilot requirements, not features already claimed as implemented.

## Submission-ready impact statement

StreamSignal helps monitoring teams turn a dated environmental signal into a transparent field question and a traceable measurement plan. Its current evidence covers weather prediction and implemented planning/recording behavior. Its proposed environmental impact—more timely and useful water-ecosystem observations under a fixed resource budget—will be tested prospectively. Human-health relevance follows through better evidence for responsible decisions, not through an unvalidated disease or contamination forecast.
