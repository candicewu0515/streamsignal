1. What this result means

A weather-group-aware plan covered 2.96 more distinct weather series per day than taking the top 5 ranked sites. Its mean selected screening index was 93.83, compared with 95.30 for the naive plan. Diversity reduced repeated weather evidence on 625 of 627 eligible days.

The uniform random-site comparator has an analytically expected coverage of 4.31 groups at the same maximum budget. This is an expectation over random subsets, not a simulated field trial, and does not prioritize anomalies.

Different weather series are not necessarily statistically independent. More series covered does not prove more pollution detected, better health outcomes or an optimal travel route. For regional selections with fewer groups than the budget, the diverse plan intentionally uses fewer visits.

3. Data denominators

106 sites share 21 distinct time series. This window has 629 calendar days and 627 days with at least one score. There are 13,167 valid group-days, 42 entirely missing group-days, and 366 scored group-days with incomplete component evidence.

2,518 / 13,167 valid group-days (19.1%) meet the selected threshold. These dependent repeated observations are counted descriptively; no confidence interval or independent-trial inference is claimed.

8. Reproducibility and limits

Scores use same-group observations from 2023–2024 within ±30 calendar days on a circular 366-day calendar. Percentiles use midrank ties. Three-day rainfall requires three known days; dry spells are unknown across a missing day until an observed wet day. Plans are ranked by index, then numeric site code; the diverse strategy caps each exact weather group at one site.

Historical health samples remain separate and are not outcome labels for this experiment. Weather-source provenance and data-publication permissions require verification. No prospective validation or field intervention has been performed.
